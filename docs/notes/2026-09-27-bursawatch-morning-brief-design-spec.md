## Intent

Build a useful autonomous Indonesian market research workflow. The morning brief is a general pre-market readout that may use Sectors when its data adds relevant evidence; its content must not be distorted to force an API call. The hackathon submission still needs a genuinely Sectors-dependent workflow, provisionally the broker-triggered issuer research card. A reader should understand the day's likely IHSG conditions and, when a broker publishes a complete swing setup, understand the issuer and the surrounding market context. The output is information and analysis, not individualized financial advice or trade execution.

The connected demo story is: scheduled morning brief -> complete broker setup arrives -> Sectors-enriched issuer research card -> after-close check of the morning call. The morning brief is the first build priority because it can be developed independently of the current BursaWatch architecture migration. The broker alert is the connected extension.

## Decisions agreed in conversation

1. **Track and connected story.** Target Automation & Workflows, Track 2. Present the scheduled morning brief and broker-triggered alert as one connected experience. Show unattended runs, timestamps, input provenance, output, and delivery evidence from the new submission itself. The submission's Sectors centerpiece remains to be selected after the morning-brief role was revised.
2. **Sectors where it contributes.** Use dated Sectors data for analysis it can actually support. Sectors is optional in the general morning brief; a missing Sectors response alone does not suppress the ordinary morning analysis. The hackathon still needs a genuinely Sectors-dependent core, provisionally the issuer research card, where a missing required Sectors response must visibly degrade that card. A decorative single API call is insufficient.
3. **Morning brief first.** Lead with an IHSG base case, supporting evidence, counterevidence, and what would change the view. Add an after-close self-audit comparing the morning scenario with the observed outcome. Do not claim a calibrated probability or win rate without measured evidence.
4. **Broker owns the setup.** A complete Phintraco setup, including its original entry, stop, targets, reasons, and chart, triggers the enriched alert. Keep those broker-authored fields and attribution intact. Sectors and other verified sources add issuer identity, sector, affiliation where supported, dated market context, corporate actions, and caution flags. Incomplete chart commentary is context, not a generated trading plan.
5. **Source roles stay visible.** Separate broker setup, Sectors context, other source claims, and system observations. Show dates, links, conflicts, missing fields, and stale data. Do not silently reconcile conflicting facts.
6. **Jev is optional and narrow.** If used, Jev classifies bounded questions such as relevance, source stance, or conflict. Deterministic code owns arithmetic, time ordering, freshness, and invariants. A generative model writes concise human-readable Indonesian text. Jev confidence is not the probability of an IHSG move.
7. **Sentiment addition.** Include a compact cross-source IHSG narrative pulse in the morning brief, including a curated X panel beyond currently watched accounts and eligible items from securities firms such as Phintraco, Tuntun, and Stockbit. Show main optimistic and cautious narratives, source links, timestamps, and disagreement with verified market evidence. One frequent publisher must not dominate. This is a view of collected commentary, not a representative poll of all traders. The earlier blanket Stockbit exclusion is superseded by the user's all-source direction; filter noisy items individually.
8. **Rotation interpretation.** The user confirmed an RRG-style four-quadrant view: leading, improving, weakening, and lagging relative to IHSG. The user selected a short-term horizon, roughly 1 to 4 weeks, for both sector and conglomerate rotation images. Arthara image references establish the intended visual style, and the supplied 22 September conglomerate CSV supplies the initial group membership. The current choice is market-cap weighting for both constructed sector and conglomerate baskets, superseding the earlier equal-weight preference. After hearing that no window is universally best, the user favored the faster 10-trading-day relative-strength and three-day momentum candidate for the several-day swing context. Basket rebalancing, sector membership, and future membership maintenance remain to be designed.
9. **Compact morning core.** The user accepted an IHSG scenario, supporting and opposing evidence, key catalysts, and a narrative pulse as the fixed text core. Corporate actions and market status appear only when their inputs are fresh, relevant, and sufficiently covered.
10. **Automatic cross-source IHSG context.** Before the morning model runs, assemble a bounded evidence bundle from eligible collected sources that mention IHSG or equivalent names. Pass that bundle with the prompt automatically, so the model can weigh source sentiment and disagreement without searching operational logs itself. This remains a design decision, not an implemented corpus or a claim of complete source coverage.
11. **One narrative pulse.** Combine relevant views from X and other collected sources into one cross-source section. Attribute each claim to its source; do not create a separate X subsection.
12. **Single-source fallback.** If only one fresh, relevant source view is available, the brief may show it as a single attributed view. Do not describe it as a cross-source pulse or imply a consensus.
13. **Three daily images.** The intended visual set is one sector rotation image, one conglomerate ("konglo") rotation image, and one IHSG view image per trading-day brief. This explicit three-image target was missing from the earlier written note. Do not fabricate an image when its underlying series, mapping, or as-of data cannot support it; state the gap. The conglomerate mapping, market-cap weighting, and initial 10/3-day windows are now selected; exact formulas and complete data coverage remain open.
14. **No qualifying source views.** Publish the morning brief without a narrative-pulse section when no fresh, relevant IHSG source view qualifies. The absence of commentary alone does not suppress the brief.
15. **Morning publication sequence, 4 October.** Publish IHSG outlook text, then its chart; sector rotation text, then its image; conglomerate rotation text, then its image. Include further sections only when relevant. Each Discord text message must fit the 2,000-character limit and remain understandable on its own. The three-image target applies to the morning brief, not the combined morning and closing publications.
16. **BursaWatch image presentation, 4 October.** Every generated image needs padding and the user's dark BursaWatch palette, with Hanken Grotesk as the only typeface. Preserve the rotation references' structure while applying the new brand rules below. Exact padding and quadrant color encoding remain open.

## Proposed morning brief content

- **IHSG scenario:** base case such as up, down, range-bound, or a more precise conditional description; key evidence and invalidation conditions.
- **Rotation:** sector and conglomerate themes in a four-quadrant view. Do not imply Sectors has a comprehensive conglomerate directory; any mapping needs an explicit source and coverage limit. Individual-stock rotation is not required for the first brief.
- **Global lead:** relevant overnight indices such as S&P 500, Nasdaq/QQQ, and KOSPI, only from verified external feeds because the reviewed Sectors documentation does not establish broad global-index coverage.
- **Today and this week:** anticipated macro or market events, expected scenarios, and source links. Distinguish a scheduled event from a speculative outcome.
- **Corporate actions:** short today/this-week dividend and other material corporate-action reminders.
- **Market status:** UMA, suspensions, and FCA entries/exits only where current source coverage is verified. Sectors suspension history alone is not a complete feed for all three.
- **Narrative pulse:** a few distinct, fresh IHSG themes from eligible collected sources, including X and securities firms, with source attribution, links, and disagreement notes. Compare claims with verified market evidence when available.
- **Research context:** relevant broker and other existing-source technical IHSG commentary when available, with attribution and freshness checks.

### Market-cap weighting reconsideration, 4 October 2026

The user subsequently asked whether both sector and conglomerate rotation baskets could use market-cap weighting by fetching market cap for each ticker, then confirmed market-cap weighting for both. This supersedes the earlier equal-weight preference. Market-cap weighting is feasible in principle: each member's weight is its dated market cap divided by the total dated market cap of eligible members, applied to subsequent normalized returns. The [Sectors daily transaction endpoint](https://docs.sectors.app/api-references/v2/indonesia/transaction/daily) documents dated close and market cap, but costs one credit per ticker query, potentially expensive across the sector universe. The [companies screener](https://docs.sectors.app/api-references/v2/indonesia/screener/companies) documents current market cap, up to 200 companies per page, and one credit per structured query. A paginated, cached market-cap snapshot could be cheaper than per-ticker daily queries if its response includes every required ticker and usable values; verify on actual responses before committing to cost or coverage. Apply weights as of a prior close, not a later snapshot, when drawing historical rotation trails. A current-only snapshot must not be backfilled into past points without labeling the approximation. Sectors may supply dated weight metadata as an explicit exception to the prior chart source boundary; chart prices remain separately sourced.

Market-cap weighting portrays where listed market value is concentrated and may bring sector baskets closer to cap-weighted index behavior, but a few large stocks can dominate each sector or conglomerate path. It also does not replicate any official free-float-adjusted index methodology automatically. The design should label the weighting method and show group coverage and the largest member's weight, so concentration remains visible. No API credits were spent for this design check.

Historical capability check on 2 October 2026: the [API reference index](https://docs.sectors.app/llms.txt) lists data endpoints, including [index daily closes](https://docs.sectors.app/api-references/v2/indonesia/transaction/index-daily), but no documented endpoint returning a rendered chart image was found. Sectors' own [visualization recipe](https://docs.sectors.app/recipes/quick-start-in-python/03-simple-moving-average) fetches JSON time-series data and draws the chart with Altair. The confirmed source boundary above supersedes the earlier suggestion to use Sectors data for locally rendered images. The existing `chart-img` use in a separate guess-stock workflow is unrelated to this morning-brief decision.

### Daily candlestick, RSI, and Pine feasibility checked 2 October 2026

The user is considering a daily candlestick with a real body and wick plus RSI for the IHSG view, without chart annotations. A full candle needs the day's open, high, low, and close; RSI also needs a history of daily closes, not only one day's values. At the 07:30 WIB premarket cutoff, the most recent complete daily candle is from the prior IDX trading session. The current Sectors [per-stock daily endpoint](https://docs.sectors.app/api-references/v2/indonesia/transaction/daily) documents open, high, low, and close for individual tickers, though open/high/low may be null. Its [IHSG index daily endpoint](https://docs.sectors.app/api-references/v2/indonesia/transaction/index-daily) documents the daily closing value only. Thus Sectors alone does not supply the documented IHSG OHLC needed for a true IHSG candle; an independently verified and permitted IHSG OHLC feed would be needed. Do not synthesize the wick or body from closes.

A read-only check on 2 October 2026 found that Yahoo Finance's publicly callable [chart feed for `^JKSE`](https://query1.finance.yahoo.com/v8/finance/chart/%5EJKSE?range=1mo&interval=1d) returned populated daily open, high, low, and close fields. This establishes technical feasibility for an IHSG candle and independently computed RSI, but does not establish a supported API contract or public redistribution rights. The independent [yfinance project](https://github.com/ranaroussi/yfinance) says it is not affiliated with Yahoo and warns that Yahoo Finance API data is intended for personal use, directing users to Yahoo's terms. Treat Yahoo as a prototype candidate while verifying use rights and reliability for a published daily image. [EODHD documents an end-of-day OHLCV API covering indices](https://eodhd.com/financial-apis/api-for-historical-data-and-volumes), but IHSG symbol coverage and display rights for this use have not yet been verified. Chart rendering remains a separate choice from sourcing OHLC data.

[TradingView's Pine documentation](https://www.tradingview.com/pine-script-docs/welcome/) says Pine runs on its servers, and its [Advanced Charts documentation](https://www.tradingview.com/charting-library-docs/latest/custom_studies/) says the embeddable libraries do not run Pine Script. A Pine community script can run on TradingView when the user has access, but cannot simply be sent to the hackathon's own chart renderer as Pine. If its method and reuse rights permit, implement the needed computation in new project code and verify its output against the original.

The user supplied local `Downloads/bursawatch/pinescripts/smc.pine` and `rsi-divergence.pine` on 2 October. `smc.pine` is an 848-line LuxAlgo Smart Money Concepts indicator with swing/internal structure, order blocks, fair value gaps, multiple timeframes, labels/boxes, and alerts. Its header declares [CC BY-NC-SA 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-nc-sa/4.0/), which requires attribution and share-alike for adaptations and limits commercial use; do not copy or translate it into the public competition repository without resolving those terms. `rsi-divergence.pine` is 174 lines and computes RSI plus regular and hidden price/RSI divergences using pivot lookback settings; no license header appears in the supplied file, so permission to copy it has not been established. The default five right-hand bars mean a pivot divergence is confirmed only after five subsequent bars. A plain RSI trace is substantially simpler than faithful divergence detection, and both require historical closes; divergence also compares price highs or lows. The practical first chart is daily candles plus ordinary RSI, with SMC and divergence deferred unless the user decides their value justifies data, fidelity, and rights work. A hosted chart-rendering API may replace local image drawing but does not itself execute these Pine files or supply missing OHLC. Its provider has not been identified.

The user-provided `rules.pdf` is a 2 October summary of the official rules, not the authority itself. The [current official rules](https://hackathon.sectors.app/rules) allow arbitrary tools, libraries, frameworks, and public open-source code that is not a finished product. They require Sectors API or MCP as a material core source, a new repository inside the build period without migrated prior-project code, and respect for third-party intellectual property. A separately sourced IHSG OHLC feed or independent RSI/chart rendering is therefore not itself barred; it does not replace the required core Sectors role. Track 2 also requires unattended scheduled or triggered execution. No project implementation is authorized by this design note.

The brief should say when a source is unavailable. It should not fill gaps by presenting old commentary as today's view. The proposed suppression of separate morning macro-news posts is still only an idea; changing an existing production destination or cadence would need its own later review and approval.

### Automatic IHSG evidence bundle, design proposal

At the 07:30 WIB cutoff, a deterministic retrieval step selects recent eligible source text using case-insensitive whole-term aliases such as `IHSG`, `JKSE`, and `JCI`, plus phrases such as `Jakarta Composite Index` and `IDX Composite`. Bare `COMPOSITE` needs context because it can refer to unrelated indices or materials. Route or topic tags may add relevant items that omit these aliases, subject to a relevance check. The user agreed that the collection window runs from the previous trading day's 07:30 WIB brief cutoff through today's 07:30 WIB cutoff, including intervening weekends and holidays. An item must have been available to the system by today's cutoff; source publication time and ingestion time remain separate so old or late-arriving commentary is not misrepresented as fresh.

"All sources" means all eligible, actually accessible source records, not an assumption that every watcher has complete searchable history. Include relevant collected commentary from Phintraco, Tuntun, Stockbit, and other available securities and market sources, regardless of whether it came from X, Telegram, WhatsApp, RSS, or another intake route.
